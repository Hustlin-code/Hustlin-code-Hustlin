What You'll Be Able To Do

Behaviour, Not Affirmations.

This course does not tell you to stay calm and trust the process. It gives you measurable procedures, written in advance, that work when your judgement is already compromised — because that is when they are needed.

1
Grade a decision separately from its result. Score your own compliance weekly, so a profitable mistake stops looking like a good week.
2
Calculate your real expectancy. Work out whether your method makes money after costs, and how long a losing streak it should be expected to produce.
3
Measure your own disposition effect. Compare how long you hold winners against losers and turn a vague suspicion into a number.
4
Size a position so you can still think. Derive size from stop distance rather than conviction, and model what a twelve-loss streak actually costs.
5
Build a drawdown response ladder. Decide in advance what triggers a size reduction, what triggers a stop, and what may never change.
6
Catch tilt from a checklist, not a feeling. Use observable behavioural tells, because self-assessment fails precisely when you need it.
7
Run a sixty-second pause protocol. A rehearsed sequence for the moment before an impulsive click, designed to work without good judgement.
8
Keep a journal that changes what you do. Pre-entry fields filled before the order, a confidence log that tests your calibration, and a violation record you can total.

Straight Answers.

Do I need to have been trading already?
It helps, but it isn't required. Several exercises ask you to look at your own trade history — if you have none, you can complete those later or work through them with a simulated record. Stage 1 assumes no prior knowledge of behavioural finance, and the arithmetic never goes beyond percentages and averages.
Is this a strategy course? Will it tell me what to trade?
No. This course contains no setups, no signals, no watchlist and no stock picks. It teaches decision-making and self-management around whatever method you already use. If you want method, the Technical Analysis and Fundamental Analysis courses cover that. Hustlin' is not a registered investment adviser and nothing here is personalised advice.
Is this therapy, or a substitute for it?
No. This is educational material about decision-making under uncertainty. It is not therapy, counselling or clinical treatment, and it is not written or reviewed by a licensed clinician. If trading is affecting your sleep, your relationships or your work, or if you find you cannot stop, that sits outside what any course can address — Stage 5 Module 8 covers this directly and points to professional resources, including the National Council on Problem Gambling helpline.
Isn't trading psychology just "be disciplined"?
That advice is accurate and useless, because discipline is the thing that fails rather than the thing that fixes it. This course is built the other way round: structural countermeasures that work without requiring good judgement in the moment. Resting stop orders rather than resolving not to move a stop. A numeric loss limit rather than knowing when to walk away. A written falsifier set as a platform alert rather than intending to stay objective.
Does it use real traders or real companies as examples?
No. Every worked example uses illustrative figures and unnamed traders. That keeps the arithmetic clean, means no number can go stale, and avoids implying that any real person's results are typical or repeatable. There are no testimonials and no account screenshots anywhere in the course.

If it has stopped being a skills problem

This course covers decision-making for people who have chosen to participate in markets. Some difficulties sit outside that, and no journal or rule set addresses them: repeatedly risking more than you planned and being unable to stop, chasing losses beyond your limits, trading money meant for rent or bills, hiding your trading, or continuing despite clear harm to your finances, work or relationships.

In the US, the National Council on Problem Gambling operates a confidential helpline on 1-800-MY-RESET (1-800-697-3738, call or text) which covers financial-market speculation, and SAMHSA's National Helpline is 1-800-662-4357. Outside the US, your national equivalent will offer similar services. There is nothing unusual about needing them.
