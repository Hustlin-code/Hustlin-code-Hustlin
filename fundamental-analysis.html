What You'll Be Able To Do

Skills, Not Stock Tips.

This course does not tell you what to buy. It teaches you a method for working that out yourself — and for recognizing when the honest answer is "I don't know."

1
Find and read any company's filings. Pull a 10-K off EDGAR, navigate to the section you need, and export the financials to a spreadsheet without retyping a number.
2
Take apart an income statement. Separate real revenue growth from acquisitions and price increases, and see exactly where a margin move started.
3
Tell profit from cash. Compare operating cash flow to net income, calculate free cash flow, and spot the divergence that shows up before trouble does.
4
Judge financial strength. Work out whether a company's debt is proportionate to its cash flows, and when the next big maturity lands.
5
Value a business three ways. Build a discounted cash flow model, run comparables, and use enterprise multiples — then reconcile when they disagree.
6
Measure a moat. Calculate return on invested capital, compare it to the cost of capital, and see whether an advantage actually persists.
7
Read the proxy. Find out what management is paid on — because that's what management will optimise for.
8
Write a thesis you'd defend. One page: what you believe, why the market disagrees, and the three specific things that would prove you wrong.

No. Stage 1 starts with what a price is, what a company filing is, and where to find one. Accounting concepts are introduced as they become necessary rather than assumed. If you can read a bank statement, you can start here.
Is this the same thing as the Technical Analysis course?
No — they answer different questions. Technical analysis studies price and volume to ask what price is doing. Fundamental analysis studies the business to ask what it's worth. Plenty of investors use both, and the two courses are designed to complement each other, but neither requires the other.
Will this tell me which stocks to buy?
No, and that's deliberate. This course teaches a method for analyzing a business yourself. It contains no stock recommendations, no watchlist, no signals, and no picks. Hustlin' is not a registered investment adviser and nothing in the course is personalised advice.

A spreadsheet and a web browser. Every filing used in the course is free on SEC EDGAR, and Stage 5 walks you through building your own scorecard and thesis page in whatever spreadsheet you already have. There is nothing to subscribe to and no data terminal required.

The worked examples use illustrative figures rather than named companies, so the arithmetic stays clean and the numbers never go stale. The action steps at the end of every module send you to real filings on EDGAR to apply it — which is where the learning actually happens.
